Each row is one real session (from /events, lightly adjusted to hit each case), drawn by today's card and by five new directions. The number beside each label is the card's height in px; green is shorter than today, orange taller. Every card is live: hover the PR rows, strips and chips, and use E's ▸ list / ▾ chips toggle. E's label also gives its height with the PRs the other way.

A. Tidy. Today's layout with the clutter taken out. The redundant status line goes, the message becomes a quoted block, agents get their own tinted tray with a live step line, and each PR is one row with a coloured status chip on the right; the whole row is the link.
  • Smallest change from today; shorter in every case.
  • Ten PRs is still ten lines.
B. Two-line PRs. Dace's idea: the title on top, then dot, number and state below, and the whole row clickable. Past four PRs it shows the first four (worst first) and "6 more".
  • Titles read in full, nothing squashed.
  • About 2× the height per PR; needs the cap to stay on screen.
C. Summed-up PRs. A strip like "1 ready · 8 drafts · 1 in review" in the PRs header, opening into the one-line list on hover. With three PRs or fewer it just shows the list.
  • The 10-PR card shrinks the most.
  • A hover-to-reveal step for the titles.
D. Two zones. The top is what it wants from you (state, question, message). A dark tray below holds the work: agents one line each (tag, live step, time), then PRs as small chips coloured by state, titles on hover.
  • Most glanceable, and the agents are clearly their own thing.
  • PR titles only appear on hover.
E. Combined (Dace's pick). D's two zones and question box. PRs collapse to D's chips under a summary header; hovering a chip puts its title in the header. The toggle (▸ list / ▾ chips) opens B's two-line rows, and it's remembered per session. Agents get more room: job, tag and time on one line, the step on up to two more, then the model (from the agent's transcript) and its type, small and dim.
  • Titles without memorising numbers, and room to read the step.
  • Tallest of the new ones with agents; an open 10-PR list is long.

Pinned sidebar

The hover card stays compact (E). On a wide window, the pin on E's card (or a click on the moth's ring while hovering it) pins one session, and its full detail opens beside the scene: nothing truncated, every PR as a two-line row grouped by state, each agent with its model, type, elapsed time, current step in full and its last few steps. The pinned moth gets a warm ring with a pin badge. Pinning another session swaps it; unpin, Escape or the pin again closes it. Below are the docked and floating variants in a 1440×900 window (scaled to fit), then the sidebar alone at full size.

The sidebar at full size

Docked (left frame). A 380px column on the right; the scene shrinks to the rest and re-centres the lantern. Nothing covers the moths, and the window reads as two panes. Needs the window at least 1200px wide so the scene keeps about 820px; below that the pin is hidden and the hover card is all there is (a pin stays remembered and comes back when the window widens).
Floating (right frame). The same content in a glass panel pinned to the top right over the full scene, as tall as it needs up to the window, scrolling inside. The scene doesn't move, but the panel covers the moths and fireflies on that side. Works down to about 980px; below that, hover card only.

Notes

What agents can tell us. Each agent sends its description (the dispatch line), its type (opus-implement, so the model comes for free), its latest activity (the last tool call's description, which already carries the task tag, like "[shader-flame] Fix halo line"), and last (when we last heard from it). The new directions pull the tag out as a chip and show the step without it. Elapsed time isn't stored yet: the server would need to record started on an agent's first event (one line in Server.swift). The times here show what that would look like.
Duplicates in today's card. Background agents show twice: once under "Running:" (from tasks) and again under "Agents". The new directions list only the tasks that aren't an agent (like "[brass-depth] Restart the preview server"). The status line repeats the pill for "Finished its turn…" and "Waiting on N background tasks", and "Sleeping until you wake it" and "Snoozed until…" repeat a sleeping or snoozed pill, so those drop. Lines that add something ("Asks: …", "Wants permission to use Bash", an API error) stay.
Model, job and type in E, without the naming convention. The model comes from the agent's own transcript (subagents/agent-<id>.jsonl): every assistant line carries message.model. Checked on Dace's real transcripts, including general-purpose and Explore agents (claude-sonnet-5, claude-opus-4-8, claude-haiku-4-5-20251001). The first one sits around line 16 to 19, about 300 KB in, because the opening lines carry the big prompt attachments, so it's one read of the file's head per agent, once, cached alongside the description loadAgentMeta already reads. That file's first line also has a timestamp, which gives a real start time for the elapsed column, even for agents found after a restart. The job is the launch description from agent-<id>.meta.json, and the type is shown as is. The values in case 1 are the real ones.
E's toggle state. Here it's a per-session flag in the page (defaulting to the list for three PRs or fewer, chips above that). The real one would live in Lantern's server settings, keyed by session, so the app, the iTerm panel and every preview agree. The toggle stops the click from reaching the card, which otherwise opens the session.
Status vs. message. Today both render as violet, weight 500, the same size. In the new directions the state line is small and dim with a glyph, the question sits in a violet-tinted box, and the "You:" message is a quote in the card's bright ink with a violet rule on its left.
